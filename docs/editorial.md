# Editorial and analytical requirements

## English style and fidelity

Use the AP Stylebook by default for generated summaries, briefings, answers, and all analytical views. Apply AP spelling, capitalization, titles, dates, numbers, punctuation, and attribution. Consult the current authorized Stylebook when uncertain; do not invent rules or treat this repository as an authorized replacement. Record market-notation exceptions in [the exception register](../config/editorial/market-notation.md).

Write neutral research prose for experienced market professionals. Do not explain obvious market connections routinely. Use specific facts, named speakers, representative short quotations, and precise attribution instead of generalized concern or sentiment. Include an AP style pass in final editing; factual accuracy, numerical precision, and evidence fidelity take precedence.

Original source text remains unchanged. Faithfully translate quotations, mark them as translations, retain original passages, and preserve numbers, units, and reference periods. Prefer quotations that reveal substantive positions or disagreements.

## Evidence distinctions

Distinguish reported facts, official statements, stakeholder positions, analyst forecasts, actual market pricing, and our interpretation. Separate rhetoric, proposals, announced decisions, enacted measures, and implementation. Qualitative political scenarios need observable signposts; numerical probabilities and directional trade calls are outside initial scope.

Local color includes regional reporting, interviews, business associations, unions, officials, and other identifiable participants. Selected voices do not establish representative market or public opinion without supporting evidence. Source selection should represent relevant positions without overstating their scope.

Show supported narrative changes across time. Preserve material disagreement; identify differing dates, stakeholder groups, and definitions. Repetition of one statement across publications is not independent confirmation. On quiet days, use ongoing themes and follows; date older background and keep unchanged previously seen material brief with links.

## Responsibilities

| Workflow | Build responsibility | Distinctions to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local Color | New domestic developments, reactions, interviews, accessible reported informal views, regional detail | Who said what, when, and in what capacity |
| Macro Outlook | Growth, inflation, monetary/fiscal policy, external accounts, financing, structural drivers | Observations, our interpretation, institutional forecasts, actual market pricing |
| Political Analysis | Policy feasibility, coalitions, institutional constraints, implementation risk, scenarios | Statements/actions; plausible scenarios/established outcomes |
| Briefing Editor | Select, reconcile, deduplicate, and write connected prose | Disagreement, evidence links, uncertainty |

Each analyst checks new evidence daily or when a briefing is requested. Scheduling must still respect budget and valid cached work. An unchanged outlook gets a brief update, not a manufactured thesis. Prior assessments provide continuity but never replace original evidence. User corrections can invalidate assessments and trigger dependent-output review.
